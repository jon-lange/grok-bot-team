# Childcare

**Group:** Home and family  
**Use:** Heavy. The busiest specialist during parental leave.

## Origin

While I was on parental leave, hiring help at home became a top priority. An agency produced zero candidates in 12 weeks. I handed the search to a specialist bot with a written job, draft-only messaging, and a hard stop before any accept, decline, interview, or pay.

An older childcare bot merged into this one. The first version polled for new messages on a timer. That burned tokens and left candidates waiting. We switched to a mail filter that forwards the hiring site's alert emails into the bot's own inbox. Each new alert wakes it. Cheaper, real time, and better for candidates.

## What it does now

Triages new applicants, sends first-round screening questions to new applicants under standing rules, keeps a tracker of who is where, and reports once per batch through the Chief of Staff. Books interviews only after I pick the times. Never publishes a live job post or pays anyone without my yes on that exact item.

## Off my plate

I no longer sit in the hiring site all day. What reaches me is a short brief, a draft, and a calendar invite. My job is the interview and the final call.

## Autonomy

**Earned:** Waking on forwarded hiring-alert email instead of polling; triaging a batch and writing one report; sending first-round screening questions to new applicants, plus per-candidate send rules for that screening loop.

**Still needs a yes:** Invites, declines, offers, calendar items, agreements, and pay. Going live with a job post. Agency or other messages outside the screening rules.
